🛡️ 1. Introduction & Core Principles

Welcome to AppLock (also referred to as Aegis Lock) — Secure App Lock & Privacy Guard (package name: com.rn.namoksha.secure.applock), developed and published by Namoksha Digital ("we", "our", or "us").

We believe your personal privacy is a fundamental human right. AppLock was engineered from the ground up on a zero-knowledge, on-device architecture. Our core mission is to empower you to lock applications, secure private media files, prevent unauthorized physical intrusions, and manage notification privacy without having to sacrifice your privacy to cloud servers.

Zero-Knowledge Principle: AppLock processes all lock credentials, app lists, private media, intruder snapshots, and scheduling profiles 100% locally on your device. This document constitutes both our comprehensive Privacy Policy and our Terms of Use. Please read it carefully to understand how your device data is handled.

📊 2. Data We Collect & Process

To deliver core security functionality, crash analytics, and optional cloud feature updates, AppLock handles data in the following distinct categories:

2.1 Data Stored Exclusively on Your Device (App-Private Storage)

  • Lock Credentials: Your PIN, Pattern, or Password is salted, hashed with cryptographic algorithms, and stored in protected, sandboxed Android app storage. Raw passwords are never retained or transmitted.
  • Biometric Authentication: Biometric verification (fingerprint or face unlock) is delegated to Android's native BiometricPrompt API and handled directly inside the device's hardware-backed Trusted Execution Environment (TEE) / Secure Element. AppLock never reads, captures, or exports biometric templates.
  • Private Vault Content: Photos, videos, documents, and audio files placed in your Vault are encrypted using hardware-backed Android Keystore + AES-256-GCM authenticated encryption. Plaintext copies are unlinked from public media galleries upon import.
  • Intruder Snapshots: When enabled, photos taken by the front camera upon repeated failed unlock attempts are saved locally with timestamps and failed attempt counts. They are never uploaded to any remote server.
  • App Lock Configurations & Schedules: The list of packages you choose to lock, auto-lock timeouts, Focus Mode profiles, and notification privacy preferences are saved locally via Android Jetpack DataStore.
  • Pro Style Preferences & Camouflage: Chosen lock screen background aesthetics, OLED black theme, acrylic blur settings, and decoy app icon configurations are stored locally.

2.2 Data Processed Automatically via Third-Party SDKs

  • Diagnostics & Crash Reporting (Firebase Crashlytics): Device hardware model, operating system version, memory status, battery level at time of crash, and anonymized stack traces to rapidly fix bugs. This data contains no personally identifiable information (PII).
  • Aggregated App Metrics (Google Analytics for Firebase): Pseudonymous, aggregated interaction metrics (such as screen visits, session frequency, onboarding completion, and feature adoption) to improve app responsiveness and usability.
  • Advertising Identifiers (Google AdMob): For non-premium users, Google AdMob may process the Google Advertising ID (AAID), coarse IP-derived geographic location (country/city level), and ad interaction telemetry for frequency capping, ad mediation, and personalized/contextual advertising (subject to user consent).
  • In-App Purchase & Subscription Tokens (Google Play Billing): Purchase tokens and subscription status indicators returned by the Google Play Store to verify premium entitlements. We do not process or store financial card numbers or bank information.

🔐 5. Private Vault & Intruder Security Architecture

5.1 Military-Grade Vault Envelope Encryption

Our Private Vault does not simply rename or hide file extensions. Instead, files imported into the Vault are secured using an advanced envelope-and-chunked AEAD (Authenticated Encryption with Associated Data) architecture:

  • Hardware-Backed Master Key: A cryptographic master key is generated inside the Android Keystore system. This key never leaves the hardware TEE (Trusted Execution Environment) or StrongBox keymaster.
  • Per-File Random Data Key: Each encrypted file is assigned a unique, cryptographically random 256-bit AES-GCM data key. The file content is encrypted with this key and broken into authenticated stream chunks with sequential nonces.
  • Key Wrapping: The file's data key is wrapped using the Keystore hardware key. Even if an attacker physically extracts the device's flash storage, ciphertext without the hardware Keystore key remains mathematically unrecoverable.
  • Gallery Cleansing: Plaintext media is securely unlinked from Android's public MediaStore once successfully encrypted into the sandbox.

5.2 Intruder Detection & Photo Snapshot Handling

When Intruder Detection is enabled by the user:

  • The app monitors sequential failed credential attempts. Once the configured threshold (e.g., 3 failed attempts) is exceeded, the front camera captures a silent snapshot.
  • The captured image is written exclusively to the app's sandboxed private storage directory (context.filesDir), invisible to public gallery apps and other applications.
  • Intruder logs and images are automatically purged according to your selected retention duration (default: 30 days) or immediately upon manual deletion with secure file overwrite.

5.3 Camouflage & Decoy Protection

AppLock provides an optional Fake Crash decoy dialog that simulates a system crash message when an unauthorized user attempts to open a protected app. Only the legitimate owner knows the secret dismissal gesture to bring up the real unlock interface.

🗑️ 9. Data Retention & Permanent Deletion

9.1 User Control & In-App Deletion

You have continuous, real-time control over all data stored by AppLock:

  • Vault Files: Decrypt and restore files back to your public gallery at any time, or delete them permanently from within the Vault.
  • Intruder Photos: Review intruder records and delete individual snapshots or wipe the entire intruder log with one tap.
  • Lock Settings & Protected Apps: Toggle off app protection or uncheck specific apps whenever you desire.

9.2 Uninstalling the App

Important Warning Regarding Uninstallation: Because all encryption keys, private vault files, and intruder snapshots reside exclusively in your device's sandboxed private storage directory (context.filesDir), uninstalling AppLock or clearing app data in Android System Settings permanently and irrevocably deletes all your encrypted vault files and intruder logs. Please restore any important media from the Vault to your public storage prior to uninstalling the app.

9.3 Excluded from Cloud Backups

AppLock explicitly configures android:allowBackup="false" and enforces custom extraction rules. This ensures that your encrypted vault, PIN hashes, and intruder photos are never inadvertently leaked to unencrypted third-party cloud backup servers.
